## Context

Most Korean branch genealogies (jokbo, 족보) were printed in small runs by the branch's own editorial committee and were never digitized. A family that knows its clan (for example "Gimhae Kim") and branch (파, *pa*) often does not know which editions exist or where to read one. Web searches mostly return clan-association pages and lineage charts, not the book.

## Guidance

1. **Collect the identifiers first.**
   - The clan's ancestral seat (본관) and surname in Hangul and Hanja.
   - The branch name in Hangul, in Hanja, and in its short and long forms. A branch is often named after its founder's pen name (호) or office, such as 판서공파 ("the Minister's branch"), so search each form.
   - The Hanja names of at least three generations of the family: self, father and grandfather. Indexes and clan offices work from Hanja names.
2. **Search the union catalogs, not just one library.**
   - **KOLIS-NET** (국가자료종합목록) covers Korean public libraries, including the National Library of Korea.
   - **RISS** covers university libraries.
   - Then check each holding library's own catalog, which shows call number, location and status.
   - Search the title in Hangul and Hanja, the compiling committee's name (…譜編纂委員會), and the publication year. Titles vary between editions: 世譜, 派譜, 族譜, with or without the founder's title.
3. **Decide whether each record is really this branch.** Match the founder's office or pen name in the title, the compiler's name, and any title printed in the page margins (版心題). When a record does not name the founder, label the match "very probably this branch" until someone checks the book.
4. **Read "online" status literally.** A disabled "view original" (원문보기) button, or a status of 오프라인 or "print only", means there is no digital copy. "None found online" means none in the searchable public web. Member-only café posts, images inside blog posts, and full-text academic databases searched only through abstracts can still hide material.
5. **Plan the reading.**
   - National Library rare-book collections are read on site with photo ID. Check current card and age rules on the library's site before going.
   - Ask whether photographing pages is allowed.
   - Interlibrary loan (책바다) may bring a copy from a participating library to a local one, at the holding library's discretion.
   - Comparing two editions shows who was added or corrected between them.
6. **Confirm the family's place in the branch.**
   - Request the pre-2008 family registers (제적등본) as a lineal descendant, at a community service centre, through the court's online family-registration service, or through a consulate abroad. They give ancestors' Hanja names and registered home (본적).
   - Check given names against the branch's generation-name chart (항렬자): each generation shares a prescribed character in a fixed position.
   - A registered home in the branch's historical area is supporting evidence, not proof.
7. **Contact the branch association** through its shrine, the county office or the local cultural centre. It may hold a later edition and can add living members to the next one.

## Why This Matters

Starting from catalog records instead of web search settles three questions quickly: whether the book exists, which editions there are, and where to read one. It also stops a researcher treating a lineage chart on a clan website as the jokbo itself. Those charts usually reproduce the jokbo's content without citing it.

## When to Apply

Use this before transcribing or matching any branch genealogy. It is also how to find a second edition to compare against one already in hand.
