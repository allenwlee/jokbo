# Finding a clan branch's jokbo

Most branch genealogies were printed in small runs by the branch's own committee and were never digitised. If you know your clan and branch but have never seen the book, library union catalogues are a faster start than web search, which mostly returns clan-association pages and lineage charts rather than the book itself.

The full guide is [docs/solutions/workflow-issues/find-a-clan-branch-jokbo-in-library-catalogs.md](../solutions/workflow-issues/find-a-clan-branch-jokbo-in-library-catalogs.md). In short:

1. Collect the identifiers first: the clan's ancestral seat (본관) and surname, the branch name in Hangul and Hanja and in its short and long forms, and the Hanja names of at least three generations.
2. Search the union catalogues (KOLIS-NET for public libraries including the National Library of Korea, RISS for university libraries), then each holding library's own catalogue.
3. Decide whether each record really is your branch, from the founder's office or pen name, the compiler and the title printed in the page margins.
4. Read "online" status literally: a disabled "view original" button means there is no digital copy.
5. Plan the visit or an interlibrary loan, and ask whether photographing pages is allowed.
6. Confirm your family's place in the branch from family registers and the generation-name chart before relying on the book.
7. Contact the branch association, which may hold a later edition.

Once you have a copy, the rest of these guides start from the scan.
