# Finding archive household records

Korean household records from the Joseon period (hojok, 호적: household registers; hogu danja, 호구단자: a household's own declaration; junhogu: an official certified extract) can confirm or challenge what a printed genealogy says about a family's place, generation and names. Many are catalogued online, but the catalogue often shows only a thumbnail, and the full image and the archive's transcription sit in a separate regional viewer.

The full guide is [docs/solutions/workflow-issues/find-full-hojok-images-and-transcripts-from-catalog.md](../solutions/workflow-issues/find-full-hojok-images-and-transcripts-from-catalog.md). It covers:

- searching the Academy of Korean Studies (AKS) catalogue by locality and document type, not only by name;
- recording each item's `dataUCI` identifier, place, date and holding institution;
- following the record to the holder's own viewer to reach the full-size image and the archive's transcription;
- checking that a download is the real image and not a thumbnail, and saving its source link and SHA-256;
- keeping the image, the archive's transcription and your own reading as separate evidence.

Five public-archive samples obtained this way, each image with its source link and each transcription beside it, are in [research/hojok-samples/](../../research/hojok-samples/). None of them is a confirmed match to a person in the Lee genealogy. A record from the right county or with the same surname is a lead, not an identity.
